[[Disruption index]]

The paper that introduced the CD index. Starting from the theoretical distinction between technologies that "depart in some deep sense from what went before" and those that build on and consolidate their predecessors, the authors argue that forward-citation counts cannot tell the two apart. Their fix is to look at how a new invention shifts later inventors' attention toward or away from the knowledge it builds on: the $CD_t$ index (direction, from $-1$ consolidating to $+1$ destabilizing) and the $mCD_t$ index (magnitude-weighted). Both are computed on U.S. patent citation data with a citation window $t$ (five years or as of 2010).

Validation runs in four exercises: correlations with known correlates of destabilizing vs. consolidating inventions, case studies of recognized breakthroughs across industries, regressions comparing impact with CD scores, and aggregate organization-level versions. Substantive finding: federal research funding pushes universities toward more destabilizing inventions, while deeper commercial ties lead to consolidating ones.

Why read it: the definitions and figures (Figure 1 walks through maximally destabilizing and maximally consolidating cases) are the clearest statement of what the [[Disruption index]] is supposed to capture, which is what the [[Embedding disruptiveness measure]] then reformulates.
